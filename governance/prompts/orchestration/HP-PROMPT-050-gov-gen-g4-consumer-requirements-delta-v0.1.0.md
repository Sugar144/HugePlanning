## Objective

Stress-test the accepted G3 logical architecture against multiple fictitious second-consumer profiles and derive the cross-project requirements delta.

Answer:

> What assumptions in the accepted logical architecture or its current HugePlanning realization fail, become ambiguous, or require parameterization when General Governance is consumed by materially different projects?

G4 evaluates consumer requirements.

G4 does not select physical architecture.

## Consumer profiles

Model exactly three fictitious consumer classes unless evidence demonstrates that two profiles are semantically redundant.

Use profiles equivalent in diversity to:

1. **Small single-repository consumer**

   * low governance ceremony;
   * one Owner;
   * one AI executor/provider;
   * small evidence volume;
   * no concurrent agent work required.

2. **AI-first concurrent software consumer**

   * multiple agents;
   * branch/worktree concurrency;
   * at least two executor/provider mechanisms;
   * delegated routine mechanics;
   * moderate evidence and review volume.

3. **Multi-team / multi-repository consumer**

   * multiple governance programs or namespaces;
   * independent reviews;
   * large historical evidence volume;
   * multiple teams/actors;
   * canonical data substantially larger than acceptable model context.

These are fictitious profiles only.

Do not identify them with real projects or import another project's canonical state.

## Controlling architecture

Stress-test the accepted G3 layers:

```text
L0  Canonical Governance Semantics / Core
L1  Configurable Cross-Project Policy
L2  Optional Governance Modules / Extensions
L3  Project-Specific Projections
L4  Provider / Executor Adapters
L5  Canonical Evidence and Historical Custody

```

Do not redesign these layers silently.

If a profile exposes a material weakness, record it as a requirements delta or architecture pressure for G5.

## Required tests

* which L0 semantics remain invariant;
* required L1 configuration parameters;
* which L2 modules are optional, required, or inappropriate;
* what must be instantiated at L3;
* executor/provider requirements at L4;
* expected evidence scale and namespace requirements at L5;
* validation/query/index requirements at L6;
* minimum viable model-facing context and navigation behavior at L7.

Explicitly test for hidden assumptions of:

* exactly one governed project;
* exactly one Owner/authority domain;
* exactly one governance program;
* exactly one artifact registry;
* exactly one current-state surface;
* globally unique un-namespaced identifiers;
* one executor/provider;
* small evidence volume;
* model-readable canonical stores;
* repository-local evidence only;
* human judgment as the only authority-boundary enforcement;
* mandatory loading of surfaces that should instead be queryable.
